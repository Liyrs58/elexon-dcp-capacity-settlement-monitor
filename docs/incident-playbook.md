# Incident playbook

## First fifteen minutes

Confirm the alert is based on fresh data. Check whether the signal is an arrival increase, a processing reduction, a data quality issue or a scheduled run condition. Record the first observation time.

## First working assessment

State the evidence in three lines. What is happening. Who may be affected. What is not known. Include the relevant BSC or service process and the operational owner.

## Questions for the service provider

Ask for interval arrivals, completions, open backlog, oldest item, latency, failed jobs, retries, capacity indicators, recent changes, dependencies and recovery estimate. Ask for definitions as well as values.

## Customer communication

Do not publish a synthetic or unverified number. Explain the confirmed service impact, any customer action, the current uncertainty and the next update time. Keep the customer channel consistent with the authorised incident owner.

## Recovery review

Compare the same measures before and after the agreed action. Check whether critical runs completed, whether the queue reduced and whether retry burden changed. Do not call the incident resolved only because arrivals fell.

## Learning record

Capture the trigger, evidence, decision, customer impact, owner, service provider input, outcome and improvement action. Link the record to the relevant process or change documentation.
